# ADR-0007: One warehouse for every competition, with v1-shaped scopes

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-05

## Context

v2 brings men's Tests, ODIs and T20Is, and the BBL, PSL, CPL and SA20 (which mostly feed the models
and players' careers). That is about 9,900 matches and 4.6 million deliveries, against the IPL's
1,243 and 0.3 million. Three things had to be decided before anything else could be built:

- **Where the data lives.** One warehouse for everything, or one per competition.
- **How a match finds its competition.** Cricsheet ships one archive per competition, but its
  recently-added feeds (which the incremental sync in V2-1 will read) mix every competition, gender
  and format.
- **How to change the foundation without breaking the IPL product**, whose export, models, reports
  and API all assume one competition, two innings and 20 overs.

## Decision

- **One warehouse** (`cricket.duckdb`) holds every competition. Players are the same people across
  competitions (Cricsheet ids are global), national sides play three formats, and the planned
  pooled T20 models need every league together. DuckDB builds all of it in about 40 seconds.
- **Matches are classified by their own details** (event name for club leagues, match type for
  internationals, men's only), configured in `config/competitions.yaml`. Archives are only download
  sources; unknown matches are left out.
- **Curated versus automatic.** The IPL is `strict`: an unknown team or venue fails the build, as
  before. Elsewhere teams and grounds outside the curated config are added automatically and listed
  for review, matches with source errors are quarantined, and cricket-logic quirks are reported as
  notes, so a new associate nation or a scorer's slip never stops a refresh.
- **v1-shaped scopes.** `criciq_pipelines.scope` copies one competition out of the full warehouse
  with exactly the v1 tables and columns (`ipl.duckdb`). Everything built before v2 reads that copy
  unchanged, and an IPL regression test compares every table of the IPL warehouse and the scored
  serving database, by content, with checksums generated by the v1 code. Consumers move to the full
  warehouse one milestone at a time.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Cross-competition questions (a player's whole career, pooled models) are plain SQL.
- The same classification serves full archives and mixed recently-added feeds.
- The IPL product was provably unchanged by the refactor; the only intended change is a
  determinism fix (a split over now belongs to the bowler who started it).

**Negative / accepted trade-offs**
- Two shapes coexist until every consumer is multi-competition, and the scope copy costs a few
  seconds per build.
- Automatically added grounds are named from Cricsheet's strings, so a few renamed grounds may
  appear twice until they are merged in `config/venue_countries.yaml`; the data-quality report lists
  them.
- Cricsheet has no Afghanistan matches, so that record is absent.

**Revisit when** the last consumer reads the full warehouse (V2-4): the scope module can then go.
